About the workshop

Why this workshop?

Continuous gravitational waves (CWs) — long-lasting, nearly monochromatic signals emitted by rapidly rotating neutron stars or exotic sources such as ultralight bosons — are one of the most exciting frontiers in gravitational wave astronomy.

This satellite workshop brings together researchers working on CW source modelling, data analysis algorithms, astrophysical inference, and detector science to exchange ideas, form collaborations, and chart the path forward for the field.

The meeting will be small and interactive, centred around contributed talks, discussion sessions, and working groups. Participation from early-career researchers is strongly encouraged.

What to expect

  • Invited review talks on key CW topics
  • Contributed talks on recent results & methods
  • Open discussion & working group sessions
  • Opportunities to propose your own talk topic at registration
  • Small & focused format (~40 participants)
  • Co-located with major IUCAA activities
Scientific scope

Topics covered

The workshop spans the breadth of continuous gravitational wave science. Below are the thematic areas we expect to cover — participants are also invited to propose additional topics via the registration form.

Neutron Star Physics

Equation of state, mountains, r-modes, and emission mechanisms

Search Methods

All-sky, targeted & directed searches; matched filtering; semi-coherent methods

Ultralight Bosons

Superradiance, boson clouds, stochastic backgrounds from boson annihilation

Detector Science

Advanced LIGO / Virgo / KAGRA sensitivity; future detectors (ET, CE)

Machine Learning

Deep learning approaches to CW searches and parameter estimation

Astrophysical Inference

Population studies, joint EM+GW observations, upper limits

Binary Systems

Accreting neutron stars, low-mass X-ray binaries as CW sources
